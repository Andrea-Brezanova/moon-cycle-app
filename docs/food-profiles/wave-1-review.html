Flagged (11)

Bean sprouts EDIT

Serving: ½ cup (USDA: 1 cup = 104 g) · raw mung bean sprouts

Add them to stir-fries and noodle dishes at the end of cooking, or to salads and spring rolls.

Cautions: Raw sprouts (alfalfa, bean or any other sprout) are on the FDA's list of foods to avoid if you are at greater risk of foodborne illness, for example during pregnancy, in older age or with a weakened immune system.

Issue: Suggests raw sprouts in salads and spring rolls; the caution (FDA: avoid raw sprouts if at greater risk of foodborne illness) is separate and easy to miss.
Proposed: Cook sprouts thoroughly if you are pregnant, older or have a weakened immune system. Add them to stir-fries and noodle dishes at the end of cooking, or to salads and spring rolls if you are not at greater risk.

Green pea EDIT

Serving: ½ cup cooked (USDA: 1 cup = 160 g) · cooked, boiled, drained, no salt

Stir into risottos, soups, pasta and curries, mash with mint, or eat from the freezer, lightly cooked. Frozen peas are a handy staple.

Issue: "or eat from the freezer, lightly cooked" is ambiguous and can read as eating frozen peas without cooking.
Proposed: Replace with: "...or cook from frozen until hot through."

Haddock EDIT

Proposed: Bake or poach in stock or water, pan-fry, or use in fish pie and chowder. (If milk is kept, say "milk (dairy allergen)".)

Mackerel EDIT

Serving: 3 ounces cooked (USDA: 3 oz = 85 g) · cooked, dry heat, Atlantic mackerel

Grill, bake or pan-fry with lemon, or serve smoked in salads and on toast. Its strong flavour suits sharp, acidic partners.

Cautions: Cook fish and shellfish to an internal temperature of 145 °F (62.8 °C). · This profile is Atlantic mackerel, a "Best Choice" for mercury. King mackerel is a different fish with the highest mercury levels, and the FDA and EPA advise avoiding it. · Fish allergen

Issue: "serve smoked in salads and on toast" suggests ready-to-eat smoked fish. Refrigerated smoked fish is a listeria concern for people at greater risk (FDA advice for pregnancy); the shown serving is cooked, not smoked.
Proposed: Grill, bake or pan-fry with lemon. (Remove the smoked suggestion, or add that smoked fish is not advised if you are at greater risk of foodborne illness.)

Blueberry CHECK

Serving: 140 g (USDA reference amount), about a generous handful · raw

Eat fresh, stir into yoghurt or porridge, blend into smoothies, or bake into muffins and pancakes. Frozen berries work well in cooking and smoothies.

Issue: "Frozen berries work well in cooking and smoothies": frozen berries are a known hepatitis A source and some authorities advise heating them for people at greater risk. The FDA/CDC wording was not fetched in this pass.
Proposed: Reviewer to confirm current official advice; either keep, or add "cook frozen berries if you are at greater risk of foodborne illness".
