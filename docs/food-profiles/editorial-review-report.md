## Food identity or variety not specified by the catalogue

54 foods

- **Almond milk** (`almond-milk`, batch 13): The calcium, vitamin D and vitamin E in this record come from fortification, and brands differ; an unfortified almond drink has far less calcium. Almond drinks contain little almond and little protein (0.4 g per 100 g).
- **Amaranth** (`amaranth`, batch 14): Cooked amaranth grain; amaranth leaves are a different food. USDA lists no folate, vitamin A, C or K values for this record.
- **Bagel** (`bagel`, batch 14): Plain enriched bagel; whole-grain and flavoured bagels differ. Iron and folate come largely from enrichment. A medium bagel is about 275 kcal and is a large serving of refined grain.
- **Beef** (`beef`, batch 7): The catalogue entry "beef" is generic. The record is 90% lean ground beef, cooked; steaks, roasts and fattier mince differ.
- **Butter** (`butter`, batch 13): Mostly fat (about 11.5 g and 102 kcal per tablespoon), mostly saturated. Salted butter; unsalted differs in sodium (not shown).
- **Canned tuna** (`canned-tuna`, batch 6): Light (skipjack) tuna, a "Best Choice" in FDA/EPA advice. Albacore (white) tuna is a different record and a "Good Choice".
- **Catfish** (`catfish`, batch 11): Farmed channel catfish, the type sold in the US. Wild and imported catfish differ.
- **Cheddar cheese** (`cheddar`, batch 13): Sharp cheddar; mild and aged cheddars differ a little. Cheese is also high in sodium and saturated fat (not shown here).
- **Chestnut** (`chestnut`, batch 10): Unlike most nuts, chestnuts are low in fat and high in starch. Raw, boiled and dried chestnuts differ; the record is roasted.
- **Chicory** (`chicory`, batch 8): The record is chicory greens; chicory root (as in coffee substitutes) is different.
- **Chocolate** (`chocolate`, batch 12): Dark chocolate 70–85% cacao; milk and white chocolate are different foods. Caffeine and theobromine are not in this nutrient set. Mineral content (iron, magnesium) comes with about 170 kcal and 12 g of fat per ounce.
- **Coconut** (`coconut`, batch 9): Raw coconut meat. Coconut milk, coconut water and coconut oil are separate foods with different composition. This is a high-fat food (about 34 g per 100 g, mostly saturated); no amount is recommended here.
- **Concord grape** (`concord-grape`, batch 9): USDA's American-type slip-skin grape is the Concord type. Grape juice is a different food.
- **Corn** (`corn`, batch 8): Sweet corn eaten as a vegetable; popcorn and cornmeal are separate foods.
- **Couscous** (`couscous`, batch 14): Standard (semolina) couscous, which is a wheat pasta and contains gluten; whole-wheat and pearl (Israeli) couscous differ.
- **Cranberry** (`cranberry`, batch 9): Raw cranberries (tart). Dried sweetened cranberries and cranberry juice are different foods.
- **Cream cheese** (`cream-cheese`, batch 13): Regular full-fat cream cheese; reduced-fat and whipped versions differ. Soft cheeses have their own safety questions in pregnancy that were not researched for this draft.
- **Date** (`date`, batch 9): Medjool dates; the existing catalogue entry does not say which variety, and deglet noor dates differ somewhat. Dates are concentrated in sugar (about 66 g per 100 g by the record's carbohydrate).
- **Dried apricots** (`dried-apricots`, batch 9): Sulfured dried apricots (the orange kind); unsulfured ones are brown and the record differs. Sulphite sensitivity is a real caution for sulphited dried fruit; the catalogue caution is carried over without an external source here.
- **Endive** (`endive`, batch 8): USDA "endive" is curly endive; Belgian endive (witloof, a chicory) is a different vegetable.
- **Fava (broad) beans** (`fava-beans`, batch 10): Dried fava beans, cooked. Fresh young fava pods are a different food.
- **Fennel** (`fennel`, batch 8): The bulb. Fennel seeds are a spice with different amounts.
- **Herring** (`herring`, batch 11): Cooked fresh herring; pickled and smoked herring (common in shops) are different and much saltier.
- **Kefir** (`kefir`, batch 7): The only USDA kefir records are branded (Lifeway); values for other kefirs differ, and live-culture content is not in a nutrient record.
- **Lamb** (`lamb`, batch 7): The catalogue entry "lamb" is generic; the record is roasted lean leg (shank half). Other cuts differ.
- **Lettuce (Bibb, Boston, romaine)** (`lettuce`, batch 3): The catalogue entry covers Bibb, Boston and romaine; this is the romaine record. Other lettuces differ.
- **Mackerel** (`mackerel`, batch 6): The catalogue entry "mackerel" is generic; the record is Atlantic mackerel. A caution about king mackerel (a different, high-mercury fish) is added.
- **Milk** (`milk`, batch 13): The catalogue entry does not say whole milk or skim; whole milk (3.25% fat) was used, and reduced-fat milks have less fat and energy but similar calcium. Raw (unpasteurised) milk is a different matter and is not covered.
- **Mushrooms (button, shiitake)** (`mushrooms`, batch 3): The catalogue entry covers button and shiitake; this is the white (button) mushroom record. Shiitake and others differ.
- **Naan** (`naan`, batch 14): Commercial plain naan; restaurant naan often contains butter or ghee and differs. USDA lists no folate, vitamin A, C or K values for this record.
- **Nut butter** (`nut-butter`, batch 5): The catalogue entry "nut butter" is generic (the existing caution says tree-nut or peanut allergen, depending on type). The record is peanut butter; almond butter and others differ.
- **Parmesan** (`parmesan`, batch 13): Grated parmesan as sold in shakers or tubs, which can include added ingredients and is lower in water than a wedge; a hard wedge has a slightly different composition. Very salty (sodium not shown).
- **Peppermint** (`peppermint`, batch 8): Peppermint tea or peppermint oil are different from the fresh leaf; a nutrient record does not cover any effect of them.
- **Persimmon** (`persimmon`, batch 9): Japanese persimmon, the common supermarket type. USDA's "native" (American) persimmon record is a different fruit with sparse data and was not used. Dried persimmon is far more concentrated.
- **Pickles** (`pickles`, batch 7): Vinegar pickles are not live-fermented; fermented pickles differ. Very high in sodium (existing caution), not shown.
- **Pine nut** (`pine-nut`, batch 10): Pine nuts as sold in shops. USDA's pinyon pine nut is a separate record.
- **Pork** (`pork`, batch 7): The catalogue entry "pork" is generic; the record is roasted lean loin. Other cuts, and processed pork, differ.
- **Rice cakes** (`rice-cakes`, batch 14): Plain brown-rice cakes; flavoured and white-rice versions differ.
- **Rye** (`rye`, batch 14): The USDA record is dry rye grain. Cooked rye berries absorb water, so a cup of cooked grain has much less per cup. Rye bread, rye flour and rye crackers are separate foods. Contains gluten.
- **Rye bread** (`rye-bread`, batch 14): Commercial US rye bread, which is often part wheat; a dense 100% rye loaf differs. Contains gluten.
- **Salmon** (`salmon`, batch 6): Farmed Atlantic salmon; wild salmon differs (less fat, less omega-3).
- **Soba noodles** (`soba-noodles`, batch 14): USDA lists no fibre value. Many soba noodles are part wheat; 100% buckwheat soba is different, and wheat content matters for gluten.
- **Sour cherry** (`sour-cherry`, batch 9): Raw sour cherries; tart cherry juice and dried cherries are separate foods.
- **Sour cream** (`sour-cream`, batch 13): Regular full-fat sour cream; reduced-fat versions differ.

- **Spirulina** (`spirulina`, batch 12): Dried spirulina. Quality, contamination and vitamin B12 forms differ between products and are not covered by this record (USDA lists B12 as 0).
- **Squid** (`squid`, batch 11): USDA also has a fried (battered) squid record with more fat and carbohydrate (175 kcal per 85 g); the raw record is used here because the catalogue does not say it is fried.
- **Tahini** (`tahini`, batch 10): USDA has several tahini records (roasted, raw, unroasted); the "kernels unspecified" one was chosen because the catalogue does not specify. Values differ between them, notably calcium.
- **Tortillas (wheat)** (`tortillas`, batch 14): Flour (wheat) tortilla; corn tortillas are a different food. Sizes vary a lot.
- **Tuna** (`tuna`, batch 11): Canned light tuna, matching the catalogue caution "prefer light/skipjack". Fresh tuna, albacore and bigeye are different records with different mercury levels; this profile does not cover them. Mercury advice for tuna was not looked up for this draft beyond the catalogue caution.
- **Turkey** (`turkey`, batch 7): Roasted breast, meat only; dark meat and deli turkey differ.
- **Vinegar** (`vinegar`, batch 12): The record is cider vinegar; other vinegars (balsamic, rice, wine) differ. A tablespoon supplies almost no nutrients. This profile makes no claim about blood sugar, weight or digestion.
- **Wheat** (`wheat`, batch 14): The catalogue entry "Wheat" does not say which wheat; USDA's durum wheat grain was used, and hard red or soft wheat berries differ somewhat. Dry, uncooked grain. USDA lists no fibre value for this record, so fibre is not shown. Contains gluten.

- **Lemon** (`lemon`, batch 9): Whole lemon without peel; most people use juice or a wedge, so the serving here is much larger than a squeeze.
- **Lime** (`lime`, batch 9): A whole lime is a larger serving than the juice or wedge people usually use.
- **Mint** (`mint`, batch 8): The catalogue has mint and peppermint separately; this is spearmint. The USDA record shows very high iron per 100 g (11.9 mg), but a normal use of mint is a few leaves, so the serving figures are small.
- **Octopus** (`octopus`, batch 11): The USDA record shows 36 µg of vitamin B12 per 100 g, about fifteen times the adult reference intake in one serving. It is the figure USDA publishes, but it is unusually high and was flagged for a second look rather than rounded or adjusted.
- **Oyster** (`oyster`, batch 6): Six oysters provide about 33 mg of zinc, over four times the reference intake in one serving. NIH ODS lists a tolerable upper intake for zinc, but that value has not been read this session: check it before publishing and consider a caution or a smaller serving.
- **Wakame** (`wakame`, batch 8): Raw (as USDA lists it); dried wakame is far more concentrated by weight. Seaweed iodine content is not in a nutrient record here; the existing catalogue caution ("Iodine: normal portions") is carried over without an external source.

## Blocked

- **Alcohol (moderate)** (`alcohol`): 'Alcohol (moderate)' is not a nutrient-offering food, and any profile would be a health-risk message that needs expert/editorial wording (Dietary Guidelines, NIAAA). Needs an owner decision on whether to profile it at all.
- **Bancha tea** (`bancha-tea`): Brewed tea has almost no nutrients in USDA records and any caffeine/fluoride/tannin message needs a sourced caution. Needs an editorial decision on format.
- **Black soybean** (`black-soybean`): No USDA record for black soybeans. The yellow soybean record (174271) is a different variety and was not substituted without owner approval.
- **Coffee** (`coffee`): Brewed coffee has almost no nutrients in USDA records; caffeine guidance (FDA, pregnancy) needs sourced wording that was not read for this project. Needs an editorial decision.
- **Decaf coffee** (`decaf-coffee`): As coffee: almost no nutrients; residual caffeine and sourcing need an editorial decision.
- **Dulse** (`dulse`): No USDA FoodData Central record for dulse (searched SR Legacy and Foundation). Needs a verified composition source before a profile.
- **Fresh-water clam** (`fresh-water-clam`): USDA has only marine/mixed clam records; freshwater clam composition differs. The catalogue marks it not ship-ready.
- **Guacamole** (`guacamole`): No USDA SR Legacy record for guacamole; composition varies by recipe (avocado, onion, lime, salt). Needs a defined recipe or a branded product.
- **Hijiki** (`hijiki`): No USDA record for hijiki. Hijiki also has a known inorganic-arsenic concern raised by food-safety agencies; a sourced caution (not read for this project) would be needed before any profile.
- **Kombu** (`kombu`): No USDA record for kombu (dried kelp). Raw kelp (168457) exists and is drafted as 'kelp', but it is a different preparation (raw vs dried) and was not used as a stand-in. Iodine content is very high and needs a sourced caution.
- **Muesli** (`muesli`): No generic USDA record for muesli (only brand cereals such as Alpen and Familia). Needs a defined product.
- **Oat milk** (`oat-milk`): No USDA SR Legacy record for oat milk; composition is brand-specific (fortification, added oil, sugar). Needs a defined product or label data.
- **Salt** (`salt`): Not a nutrient-offering food: salt supplies sodium (and iodine only if iodised), and sodium guidance (e.g. the Dietary Guidelines limit) is a safety/limit message, not a 'what this food offers' profile. Needs an editorial decision on format before any draft.
- **Seitan** (`seitan`): No USDA record for seitan. Values depend on the product (vital wheat gluten content, added soy sauce, salt). Needs a defined product.
- **Soft-shell crab** (`soft-shell-crab`): Soft-shell crab is blue crab caught after moulting and eaten whole, usually fried. USDA has cooked blue crab (174205) but not soft-shell crab, so the preparation and whole-animal composition differ. Needs a decision on whether blue crab may stand in.
