# Draft batch 6: seafood (21 foods; nothing here is published)

clam, mussel, oyster, sardine, anchovies, scallop, trout, canned salmon, canned light tuna,
haddock, pollock, crab, shrimp, sea bass, salmon, cod, flounder, halibut, mackerel, snapper,
tilapia. USDA records read 27 Oct 2026; same method and checks as earlier batches.

## Identity questions
- **Sea bass:** ambiguous (black sea bass is an FDA/EPA Best Choice, Chilean sea bass a Good
  Choice). The record is "sea bass, mixed species".
- **Mackerel:** the record is Atlantic mackerel; a caution about king mackerel is added.
- **Red snapper:** the record is snapper, mixed species.
- **Crab:** Alaska king crab. **Salmon:** farmed Atlantic (wild differs). **Tuna:** light
  (skipjack) in water; albacore is a different record and a Good Choice.
- **Oyster:** six oysters give about 33 mg of zinc, over four times the reference. NIH ODS has
  a tolerable upper intake for zinc that I have not read this session: check it and decide
  on a caution or a smaller serving.

## Cautions (sourced this session)
- Mercury: FDA/EPA "Advice about Eating Fish" lists (page read 27 Oct 2026, content current as
  of 03/05/2024): halibut and snapper are Good Choices; the advice for people who are or might
  become pregnant, or are breastfeeding, is 2–3 servings a week of Best Choices or 1 of Good
  Choices. The existing catalogue wording on halibut is kept and now cites this page.
- Cooking temperature, USDA FSIS Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart (read 27 Oct 2026):
  fish and shellfish 145 °F (62.8 °C). Added to cooked fish and shellfish.
- Existing catalogue cautions (allergens, "cook thoroughly", raw oysters) are all carried over.
- No fish got a caution beyond these; Best Choice fish need none under that advice.

## Things to know
- Vitamin B12 percentages are very large for shellfish (clams about 3,500%); they are accurate
  for the record (a reference intake of 2.4 µg) but may look odd. You may prefer to show the
  amount only.
- Anchovies are very salty; sodium is not shown and no salt caution has a source yet.

## Unsourced wording to accept, change or remove
Cooking tips (discard shells that stay closed, searing, steaming), "lean", "mild", "sweet",
and the iron-absorption comparison (the plant-iron statement is NIH ODS; the shellfish
comparison is its flip side).
