### Beef (`beef`)

*Featured phase in the catalogue:* menstrual (editorial; see part 5).  *Category:* protein.

**Nutritional facts (sourced)**

- Record: USDA FoodData Central 171793, "Beef, ground, 90% lean meat / 10% fat, patty, cooked, pan-broiled" (SR Legacy, 4/1/2019); preparation: cooked, pan-broiled patty (90% lean)
- Serving: 3 ounces cooked (USDA: 3 oz = 85 g)
- Per serving: 173 kcal, protein 21.4 g, fat 9.1 g, carbohydrate 0.0 g, fibre 0.0 g. Key nutrients: Vitamin B12 2.4 µg (99% of the reference), Zinc 5.4 mg (67% of the reference), Iron 2.4 mg (13% of the reference), Protein 21.4 g.
- Allergens (catalogue tags): none tagged

**Five-part text**

1. *What this food offers:* Cooked 90% lean ground beef is a dense source of vitamin B12 (about 99% of the reference in 3 ounces), zinc (about 67%) and iron (about 13%), with about 21 g of protein.
2. *A practical serving:* 3 ounces cooked (USDA: 3 oz = 85 g)
3. *How to eat it:* Brown for sauces, chilli and tacos, shape into burgers, or mix with grated vegetables or lentils to stretch it. Pair with vitamin C foods such as tomatoes or peppers.
4. *Cycle relevance:* Iron needs are higher for women aged 19 to 50 (18 mg a day, against 8 mg for men), partly because of menstrual blood loss (NIH ODS). Iron from meat is absorbed more readily than iron from plant foods, so beef is an efficient source; 3 ounces provide about 2.4 mg. No phase-specific claim is made.
5. *Good to know (cautions):* Cook steaks, chops and roasts to 145 °F (62.8 °C) and let them rest for at least 3 minutes; cook ground meat to 160 °F (71.1 °C). [sourced]

## 5. Evidence for each phase-specific statement

**There are no phase-specific statements in the Wave 1 profile text.** The profiles describe what a food provides and, in some, a nutrient fact for people who menstruate. The only phase element is the catalogue's "featured phase" for each food, which is editorial:

- The product principle (`docs` in the research project, and the in-app note) is that a featured phase is where Moon Cycle chooses to introduce a food for nutritional fit, meal style and monthly variety, "not a claim that the food should only be eaten during that phase".
- I found **no** phase-specific nutritional requirement for any Wave 1 food. The design review rated almost all original phase links "editorial", and even its strongest case (iron) says eating iron-rich food during the period "hasn't been shown to matter more than at other times".
- Where origin is concerned, see the provenance audit (PR #63): the original phase lists have no identified independent source.
- Wave 1 foods by featured phase: menstrual 20, follicular 24, ovulatory 23, luteal 34.

## 6. Remaining substantive questions

1. **Approve or amend the cycle-relevance wording** (part 2). Two points to consider: it says "women aged 19 to 50" because the NIH references are for adult women; and it names menstrual blood loss, which applies to anyone who menstruates.
2. **Featured phase and provenance.** Wave 1 profiles belong to foods whose featured phase has no independent source (PR #63). Do you want profiles published before the phase lists are re-derived, given the profile text itself makes no phase claim?
3. **Allergen lines.** They come from catalogue tags (conservative). Keep them all as written (recommended) or refine with an allergist or label source (decision D12).
4. **Sodium** is data only; Food Detail has no place for a note (decision D11). No Wave 1 profile has a sodium note.
5. **"If your periods are heavy…"-style advice** is not used in Wave 1 text; the five approved profiles use a version of it. Confirm that is intended.
6. **Reviewer and date** to record on each approved profile.
