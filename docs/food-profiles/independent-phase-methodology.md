# An independent, evidence-informed method for food suggestions across the cycle

**Status: adopted by the owner on 29 Oct 2026 for internal editorial use (the four-class evidence system and its uncertainty labels). The algorithm outline is not implemented and the phase mappings are unchanged.** Nothing here changes the app, the catalogue or any phase assignment. It does not use, reproduce or reconstruct any other author's food chart or framework; it starts from nutrition science and from MoonCycle's own data (USDA FoodData Central profiles, NIH reference intakes).

## 0. What this document does not say

- **No assignment is clinically established or independently validated.** Not one of the 142 phase assignments is represented as clinically established, independently validated, or scientifically proven to matter in a particular phase. They are editorial suggestions for variety.
- **Uncertainty labels** used in every internal record: *Established* (class 1), *Plausible, unproven* (class 2), *Traditional, no clinical evidence* (class 3), *Not supported* (class 4), and *Not assessed* for anything not yet read. A statement without a label is not ready to use.
- No professional review has taken place and none is claimed.

## 1. Principles

1. **Nutrition first, cycle second.** Most nutritional needs do not change by the day of the cycle. Suggestions should be good general nutrition, with the cycle used only where there is a stated, sourced reason.
2. **Four kinds of statement, never blended.** Every statement shown to a user belongs to exactly one class below and is worded accordingly.
3. **A featured phase is not a benefit claim.** It is a way to introduce variety over a month. The app already says "These are suggestions, not rules".
4. **No hormonal or fertility promises.** No "balances", "supports", "boosts", "detoxes" or "regulates" language about hormones, ovulation, fertility, PMS or mood.
5. **Safety and exclusions come first.** Allergens, diets and food-safety cautions filter or qualify every suggestion before any ranking.
6. **Everything has a traceable source and a date.** If it cannot be sourced, it is not shown.

## 2. The four classes

| Class | Definition | May say | Must not say |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Established nutritional fact** | Supported by authoritative guidance or consistent high-quality evidence (NIH ODS, USDA data, Dietary Guidelines, Cochrane-level reviews) | "A serving provides X"; "People who menstruate lose more iron (NIH ODS)" | That it matters more in one phase unless that is shown |
| **2. Plausible but unproven phase-related consideration** | A reasonable mechanism or observed association, with limited or mixed evidence | "Some research suggests…; the evidence is limited" | Cause, cure, treatment, or that anyone should change what they eat |
| **3. Traditional dietary association** | A long-standing practice or convention without clinical support | "Traditionally associated with…; no clinical evidence" (and only on request) | Any health effect |
| **4. Claim lacking credible support** | No reliable evidence, or evidence of no effect | Nothing. Not shown. | Anything |

## 3. Research evidence matrix

Evidence quality: **High** (systematic review/RCT evidence consistent), **Moderate**, **Low**, **Very low**, **Authoritative guidance** (reference values). "Read" means I read the abstract or page for this proposal; nothing here is taken from secondary blogs.

| # | Topic | What the evidence says | Source (read) | Quality and limits | Class | How MoonCycle may use it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Iron and menstruation | "Losses are greater in menstruating women because of blood loss"; women with heavy menstrual bleeding are a group at risk of inadequate iron. RDA for women 19 to 50 is 18 mg/day (men 8 mg) | NIH ODS, Iron fact sheet for health professionals (updated 4 Sep 2025) and for consumers | Authoritative guidance. It supports a daily need, not a phase-timed one | **1** (need exists); timing within the cycle: no evidence found | "Iron needs are higher for people who menstruate", on any food rich in iron, any day |
| E2 | Vitamin C and plant (non-haem) iron | Vitamin C eaten in the same meal improves absorption of plant iron | NIH ODS, Iron fact sheet for consumers: "Your body absorbs iron from plant sources better when you eat it with… foods that contain vitamin C" | Authoritative guidance | **1** | Pairing suggestion in recipes and Build a Meal |
| E3 | Energy intake across the cycle | Energy intake is higher in the luteal than the follicular phase: 15 datasets, 330 women, SMD 0.69 (P = .039), a crude 168 kcal/day; authors note repeated methodological inconsistencies | Nutrition Reviews 2025, systematic review and meta-analysis (PMID 39008822) | Moderate-to-low: self-reported intake, varied phase definitions, healthy young adults only | **2** (an average observation, not a need) | At most: "Appetite often rises in the luteal phase for some people; satisfying, fibre-rich meals are a reasonable choice" |

| E6 | Dietary supplements for period pain | 27 RCTs, 3,101 women: "no high quality evidence to support the effectiveness of any dietary supplement"; evidence low or very low quality for all comparisons; safety evidence lacking | Cochrane, Dietary supplements for dysmenorrhoea (Pattanittum et al., 2016; CD002124) | High-quality review, finding low-quality primary evidence | **2/4** (no supplement is supported) | Do not suggest foods as period-pain remedies |

| E10 | Protein, fibre, magnesium, calcium, essential fats, other nutrients | Reference intakes exist (NIH ODS, Dietary Guidelines). In the sources read for this proposal I found **no** evidence that requirements change by cycle phase | NIH ODS fact sheets used in the profiles; no phase-timing source found | Authoritative for amounts; absence of phase-timing evidence is an absence of evidence in what I read, not proof | **1** (amounts); phase timing: none identified | Use amounts only |

Items I did not verify from a primary source this session (so they carry no weight here): magnesium and vitamin B6 for PMS, a later pair of calcium trials reported as negative, and cruciferous vegetables and oestrogen metabolism.

## 4. Proposed suggestion algorithm and explanation system

The cycle is an input to *presentation* and *variety*, not to nutritional need.

**Inputs.** The user's exclusions (allergens, diets, food avoidances), the phase currently being viewed, and the food profile data (USDA-based amounts, cautions).

1. **Hard filter (non-negotiable).** Remove any food that conflicts with an allergen, diet or avoidance. Apply food-safety qualifiers. This step never depends on phase.
2. **Pool.** All remaining foods are eligible in every phase. A featured phase makes a food more *visible*, never exclusive.
3. **Foundation score (phase-independent).** For one serving: coverage of the references that matter for most adults eating a varied diet (iron, folate, calcium, magnesium, potassium, fibre, protein, omega-3), capped per nutrient so one nutrient cannot dominate. Penalise nothing for being "low" in a nutrient; a food can be suggested for flavour, texture or fit.
4. **Variety.** Prefer foods and categories not suggested in the last 7 days.
5. **Practicality.** Recipe availability, season where known, and the user's saved items.
6. **Optional phase lens (class 2 only, off by default for scoring).** During days of bleeding: surface iron-rich foods and vitamin C pairings, because the iron need is real, framed as "good any day, and a sensible choice now". In the luteal phase: surface satisfying, fibre-rich options, framed as a possibility ("some people notice a bigger appetite"). Follicular and ovulatory phases have no nutrient rationale and rely on variety.
7. **Explain.** Every suggestion carries a one-line reason built from templates:

| Class | Template | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Fact | "{Food} provides {amount} of {nutrient} per serving ({source})." | "A serving of lentils provides 3.3 mg of iron (USDA). People who menstruate need more iron (NIH ODS)." |
| Variety | "Featured this phase for variety. Any food can be enjoyed in any phase." | Shown on every featured food |
| 2 Plausible | "Some research suggests {x}; the evidence is limited." | "Appetite is often higher in the luteal phase (a review of 15 studies, with mixed methods)." |
| 3 Traditional | Only on request: "Traditionally associated with {x}; no clinical evidence." | Not shown by default |
| 4 | Not shown | |

8. **Never output** hormone, fertility, PMS, mood or "balance" language, and never rank a food higher because a phase list contains it.

**Why this is independent.** The ranking uses nutrient data and exclusions; the phase affects only wording and visibility, and only where a sourced class-2 reason exists.

## 5. Using the existing 142 phase assignments responsibly

The assignments stay as they are until you approve an implementation plan. Until then:

1. **Describe them only as featured suggestions for variety.** Never as a nutritional requirement or a hormonal fit. The profile text already follows this.
2. **Do not feed them into any recommendation as evidence.** In Build a Meal 2.0, treat phase as a soft preference at most, never a filter.
3. **Give each assignment a recorded rationale class.** `phase-independent-assessment.json` classifies all 142: R1 nutrient-informed variety (98), R2 variety and meal-fit (40), R3 traditional association (4). **None is classed as scientifically supported as phase-specific**, and none is proposed for change.
4. **Treat the four seed foods as an owner decision**: keep them as ordinary variety suggestions with no claim, or drop the pairing.
5. **Do not change a phase just to look different.** A change is warranted only when a documented reason (nutritional, safety, practicality) exists for that food.
6. **Re-derive gradually, not wholesale.** Start with foods where a documented reason could differ from the current phase (for example a food featured in a phase while its profile gives a reason elsewhere) and decide food by food.

## 6. Independence safeguards

- Decide from the evidence matrix and the nutrient data, not from anyone's published food lists.
- Keep a dated decision log: food, decision, class, sources, who decided.
- Do not paste or paraphrase another publisher's phase lists, headings or taglines into notes or code.
- Keep the evidence (source pages, dates) with each decision.
- Whether the method needs legal review is a question for you and a qualified adviser; this document gives none.

## 7. Unresolved scientific questions

1. Do any nutrient requirements (not appetite) vary by cycle phase in healthy women, and by how much?
2. Does the *timing* of iron intake within the cycle change iron status, or only the total over time?
3. Is the luteal rise in energy intake (about 168 kcal/day on average) large enough, and consistent enough across people, to justify any advice?
4. Does food calcium (as opposed to a 1,200 mg supplement) affect premenstrual symptoms? Do later trials agree with the 1998 trial?
5. Are the benefits reported for flax or sesame specific to those seeds, to their lignans or fibre, or to the phase rotation?
6. Which culinary amounts, if any, of ginger or other foods relate to period pain (the trials use supplement doses)?
7. How should evidence from people with PMS, PCOS or dysmenorrhoea be applied to people without them?
8. How much do hormonal contraception, irregular cycles, perimenopause and pregnancy change all of the above? (The app should not assume a regular, unmedicated cycle.)
